 0. The brutally honest verdict


 This is a high-risk, high-reward SIH problem. A strong, defensible prototype is feasible. A universal system that
 reconstructs every hidden facade and occluded surface accurately from any arbitrary single straight-line video is not
 physically possible.

 The winning version should therefore be framed as:

      A trustworthy, georeferenced single-pass reconstruction system that produces metrically useful 3D geometry for
      directly observed surfaces, uses AI to improve difficult observations, explicitly labels inferred or unseen regions,
      and attaches an accuracy and confidence report to every output.


 Do not frame it as:

      Upload any video and AI magically creates a perfectly complete, survey-grade 3D world.


 The second claim is easy for a photogrammetry or robotics evaluator to destroy.

 Go / no-go assessment

   Dimension                                                          Honest assessment

   Prototype feasibility                                              Yes, if the first target is a 30-90 second controlled video and 80-200 selected keyframes

   Strong visual demo                                                 Yes

   Metric measurement on visible surfaces                             Yes, with calibration, parallax, georeferencing, and independent validation

   Survey-grade absolute accuracy using ordinary GPS alone            No

   Complete unseen-surface reconstruction                             No; only labelled inference is possible

   Near-real-time sparse preview                                      Feasible

   Near-real-time final dense mesh from 4K video                      Hardware- and scene-dependent; not the first promise

   Training a foundation model from scratch                           Neither necessary nor realistic for SIH

   Building a differentiated product instead of a wrapper             Feasible, but requires confidence, fusion, validation, and a serious UI

 The project's central differentiator

 Every reconstructed element must have provenance:
 1.   Observed - high confidence: supported by several views, healthy triangulation angle, low reprojection error, and
      consistent depth.
 2.   Observed - medium confidence: visible but weakly constrained.
 3.   Observed - low confidence: marginal geometry; measurement warning required.
 4.   AI-inferred: plausible completion, never treated as measured.
 5.   Unseen: no geometry claim.

 Measurements should default to observed geometry only. If a user attempts to measure across inferred or
 low-confidence geometry, the application must warn them and show the expected uncertainty.

 1. What the problem is actually asking us to solve


 The input is not just a video. It is a synchronized sensor package:
  -  1080p or 4K drone video.
  -  GPS coordinates sampled during the pass.
  -  Flight metadata.
  -  Optional IMU, barometric altitude, camera intrinsics, and RTK/PPK corrections.

 The useful output is not just a pretty render. It must support:
  -  Visualization.

  -  Distance, height, slope, area, and possibly volume measurement.
  -  Spatial analysis.
  -  Georeferencing.
  -  Export into standard 3D and geospatial tools.
  -  A statement of accuracy, coverage, and uncertainty.

 The eight engineering problems inside the statement
 1.  Decode and synchronize video and telemetry.
 2.  Select useful frames from a highly redundant video.
 3.  Recover camera intrinsics, camera trajectory, and sparse geometry.
 4.  Fuse noisy GPS/IMU priors without letting bad telemetry corrupt visual geometry.
 5.  Estimate dense depth and create a point cloud or mesh.
 6.  Remove or suppress dynamic objects and transient artifacts.
 7.  Georeference, scale, orient, validate, and quantify uncertainty.
 8.  Deliver the result through a usable, secure, mostly offline application.

 The hardest physical limitations
  -  A surface that never appears in any frame cannot be measured.
  -  A nearly straight camera trajectory is geometrically weak for transverse depth and can become degenerate.
  -  Rooftops and facades compete for camera angle in a single pass.

  -  Ordinary GNSS can be wrong by several metres, especially near buildings and vegetation.
  -  Monocular reconstruction has an arbitrary scale until a metric prior is introduced.
  -  Rolling shutter, electronic stabilization, autofocus, or digital zoom can make camera intrinsics vary during the video.
  -  Video compression destroys fine features and creates block artifacts.
  -  A visually impressive neural render is not automatically a metrically reliable model.

 The public GPS.gov accuracy guidance notes that ordinary receiver accuracy depends on geometry, blockage,
 multipath, atmosphere, and hardware; high-end augmented systems are needed for centimetre-level positioning. This is
 why the application must consume telemetry uncertainty rather than treating every GPS sample as exact truth.

 Product statement

 The prototype accepts one drone video plus a telemetry file, generates a georeferenced 3D reconstruction, exposes
 measurable observed geometry in a browser, highlights weak/inferred/unseen regions, and produces a
 machine-readable quality report.

 Primary demo scenario
  - One campus building, courtyard, road segment, or construction scene.
  - 30-90 seconds of video.
  - 4K preferred; 1080p supported.
  - Fixed focal length and stable focus.
  - One continuous pass with a useful oblique angle.
  - GPS/SRT/CSV or MAVLink telemetry.
  - 80-200 retained keyframes after quality filtering.
  - Several independent surveyed or tape/laser-verified check measurements.

 P0 capabilities - non-negotiable
  - Upload video and telemetry.
  - Inspect codec, resolution, duration, frame timestamps, and metadata.
  - Parse at least CSV and DJI-style SRT; add MAVLink .tlog if available.
  - Synchronize telemetry to frame timestamps with a visible offset control.
  - Score blur, exposure, redundancy, and motion.

  - Select keyframes automatically and let the operator review them.
  - Calibrate or ingest camera intrinsics.
  - Mask dynamic classes or exclude frames with severe motion.
  - Run sequential structure-from-motion.
  - Incorporate GPS position priors with uncertainty.
  - Generate a sparse point cloud and camera trajectory.
  - Generate a dense point cloud for observed surfaces.
  - Georeference into a local metric CRS and retain WGS84 metadata.
  - Show the model, flight path, source frames, and confidence layer.
  - Measure distance and height on high-confidence observed geometry.

  - Export PLY/LAZ and a project quality report.
  - Preserve logs and make every run reproducible from a saved configuration.

 P1 capabilities - finalist-quality target
  - Textured mesh export as OBJ and/or glTF/GLB.

  - DSM and orthomosaic for predominantly downward-looking scenes.
  - AI-assisted feature matching fallback in weak texture.

     estimates about 16 GB for roughly 250 images and 32 GB for roughly 500 images, but those are planning figures,

     recommendations.

 5. End-to-end reconstruction pipeline

 Stage 0 - Project creation and immutable ingest

 Inputs
  - Video.
  - Telemetry.
  - Optional intrinsics, IMU calibration, RTK/PPK corrections, ground truth.

 Actions
 1.  Compute SHA-256 for every uploaded file.
 2.  Save original names, byte sizes, MIME types, and timestamps.
 3.  Probe the video using ffprobe.

 4.  Reject unsupported or corrupt inputs early.
 5.  Parse the telemetry into a common schema.
 6.  Validate coordinate and time ranges.
 7.  Create a project manifest and a run ID.

 Required output
  - ingest_report.json.
  - Normalized telemetry parquet/CSV.
  - Video stream metadata.
  - Warnings such as variable frame rate, missing timestamps, unknown altitude datum, or uncertain stabilization.

 Acceptance criteria
  - No reconstruction begins when frame timing is unknown or telemetry is structurally invalid.
  - Every user-visible warning is also machine-readable.

 Stage 1 - Time synchronization
 Problem
 The camera clock and flight-controller clock may have an offset, drift, or different epoch. A one-second offset at 8 m/s
 produces an 8 m camera-prior error.

 Initial method
 1.  Convert video frame presentation timestamps into seconds from video start.
 2.  Convert telemetry time into seconds from telemetry start.

 Stage 2 - Camera calibration and image model
 Preferred order
 1.  Use trusted manufacturer/lab intrinsics for the exact recording resolution and mode.

 2.  Use a team-generated ChArUco/checkerboard calibration.
 3.  Use shared-intrinsics self-calibration in COLMAP.
 4.  Fall back to a simple radial model with an explicit warning.

 OpenCV recommends multiple views of a calibration target and notes that a roughly printed, unmeasured board can
 itself introduce error. See the OpenCV calibration tutorial.

 Calibration capture checklist
  - Same resolution, crop, focus, and stabilization mode as the flight.
  - At least 15-30 useful board views across the frame.
  - Varied board orientation and distance.
  - Board dimensions physically measured.
  - No motion blur.
  - RMS and per-view reprojection error retained.

 IMU/camera calibration
 If a rigid camera-IMU setup and raw IMU are used, Kalibr can estimate spatial and temporal calibration using an
 AprilGrid. See Kalibr. This is a P2 task unless the team already has synchronized sensor hardware.

 Rolling shutter
  - Record readout time if available.
  - Prefer a slower flight and fast shutter.
  - Do not confuse fast shutter with global shutter; geometric row timing still exists.
  - Add rolling-shutter compensation only after a controlled experiment proves it is a major error source.

 Stage 3 - Frame extraction and quality scoring

 Why not use every video frame
 Thirty frames per second creates extreme redundancy, excessive matching cost, and almost-zero baselines between
 neighbours. Useful keyframes should preserve overlap while gaining enough camera motion for triangulation.

 Per-frame signals
  - Timestamp.
  - Blur score.

  - Saturated/dark pixel fraction.
  - Entropy or local contrast.
  - Compression/blockiness indicator.
  - Optical-flow magnitude.
  - Motion direction consistency.
  - GPS displacement since last retained frame.
  - Attitude change.
  - Scene-change/appearance difference.
  - Dynamic-object coverage.

 Selection policy
 Use an adaptive selector rather than fixed N fps alone:
 1.  Start from a maximum candidate rate, such as 5 fps.
 2.  Reject severe blur and exposure failures.
 3.  Retain a candidate when median optical flow, GPS displacement, or rotation passes a minimum baseline.

 4.  Reject it if overlap with the previous retained keyframe is too low.
 5.  Preserve extra keyframes around turns, occlusions, or sudden appearance change.
 6.  Cap long static segments.
 7.  Ensure end-of-pass coverage.

 Important implementation detail
 Blur thresholds must be calibrated per resolution and scene. A universal variance-of-Laplacian threshold is brittle. Begin
 with distribution-based thresholds, manually review the chosen/rejected frames, and save contact sheets.

 Outputs
  - Original selected frames.
  - Matcher-resolution frames.
  - Thumbnail/contact sheet.
  - frame_scores.parquet.

 Stage 6 - Visual reconstruction with GPS priors
 Primary reconstruction
 Use COLMAP pose_prior_mapper or PyCOLMAP equivalent:
  -  Insert each keyframe's position prior.

  -  Convert geodetic coordinates to a stable local metric frame first.
  -  Set prior covariance from reported GNSS accuracy/HDOP where possible.
  -  Otherwise use conservative default standard deviations.
  -  Share/fix intrinsics appropriately.
  -  Use robust bundle adjustment.

 Coordinate handling
 1.  Keep original WGS84 latitude/longitude/height.
 2.  Select a local origin near the flight.
 3.  Transform to ECEF and then local ENU, or an appropriate UTM/project CRS.
 4.  Run numerical geometry close to the origin to avoid precision loss.
 5.  Store the complete transform chain.

 PROJ is the standard transformation engine to use for CRS conversions. Never perform metric measurements directly
 in latitude/longitude degrees.

 Why GPS is a soft prior
  -  Basic GNSS is noisier than relative visual structure.
  -  Urban multipath can create biased positions.
  -  Vertical GNSS is usually worse than horizontal.
  -  A bad prior can bend a reconstruction if overweighted.

 The solve should compare at least:
  -  Visual-only SfM followed by robust Sim(3) alignment to GPS.
  -  Joint mapping with GPS position priors.

 Choose the better method using held-out checkpoints and residuals, not aesthetics.

 Outputs
  -  Registered cameras.
  -  Sparse points and tracks.
  -  Reprojection residuals.

 Vegetation handling

  - A lower-confidence observed surface/occupancy.
  - Optional semantic class.

 Stage 9 - AI depth prior and weak-region assistance

 Correct use
 For a retained frame, compute learned metric/relative depth and align it to reliable multi-view depth using robust
 scale-and-shift or metric calibration. Use it to:
  - Flag holes likely caused by weak texture.
  - Propose depth where multi-view support is marginal.
  - Improve initialization or regularization.
  - Estimate a weak-region uncertainty signal.

 Fusion gate
 A learned depth sample can influence observed geometry only when:
  - It agrees with reliable multi-view depth in neighbouring pixels/frames.
  - It is cross-view consistent after reprojection.
  - It is outside dynamic masks.
  - Its uncertainty is below a calibrated threshold.
  - It does not contradict camera/GPS geometry.

 Otherwise it remains a visual prior or becomes AI_INFERRED.

 Never do this
  - Convert one monocular depth frame into a point cloud and call it accurate.

 Stage 12 - Confidence and provenance
 Per-point or per-cell features
  -  Number of supporting views.
  -  Track length.

  -  Triangulation angle.
  -  Reprojection error.
  -  Depth photometric score.
  -  Multi-view geometric consistency.

  -  Dynamic-mask proximity.
  -  Learned-depth disagreement.
  -  GPS/pose residual in supporting cameras.
  -  Whether meshing filled a hole.

 Initial confidence engine
 Use transparent rules and thresholds calibrated on a held-out scene. Do not invent an unexplained "AI confidence of
 97%."

 Example status logic:

 if generated_by_completion or unsupported_mesh_fill:
     status = AI_INFERRED
 elif views >= 4 and reprojection_error is low and triangulation_angle is healthy
      and multiview_consistency is high and not near_dynamic_mask:
     status = OBSERVED_HIGH
 elif views >= 3 and consistency is acceptable:
     status = OBSERVED_MEDIUM
 else:
     status = OBSERVED_LOW
 Later calibration
 Train or calibrate a lightweight confidence model against point-to-reference errors. Inputs are the features above; output
 is the probability that a point/measurement lies within a declared tolerance. Use reliability diagrams and expected
 calibration error.

 Measurement policy

  -  Yellow: allowed with warning and uncertainty.
  -  Red/low: explicit confirmation.
  -  Inferred: disabled for "verified measurement" mode.

 9. Single-pass flight and capture SOP


 This is for lawful, authorized test capture only. Before flying in India, verify the current airspace zone and applicable
 permissions under the Ministry of Civil Aviation's Drone Rules and amendments. Do not collect sensitive sites without
 authorization.

 Scene selection
  -  Obtain site permission.
  -  Avoid airports, restricted zones, crowds, and sensitive installations.
  -  Prefer a rigid, textured structure for the first test.
  -  Avoid glass-dominant or repetitive facades initially.
  -  Mark independent checkpoints before flight.
  -  Record ground-truth measurements and photos.

 Camera settings
  -  4K/30 fps preferred.
  -  Fixed focal length.
  -  Lock focus.
  -  Disable digital zoom.

  -  Prefer mechanical gimbal over electronic warping.
  -  Use fast daylight shutter, approximately 1/800-1/1600 s as a starting range, then verify exposure.
  -  Keep ISO low enough to preserve feature detail.
  -  Avoid clipped sky and crushed shadows.
  -  Avoid changing picture profiles mid-flight.
  -  Record original files without messaging-app recompression.

 Path geometry
  -  One continuous pass.
  -  Maintain stable speed.
  -  Use an oblique angle that sees both facade and roof where the target demands both.
  -  Avoid a perfectly featureless, purely collinear view if the operational definition allows a slight smooth curve within the
     single pass.
  -  Do not orbit the building and call it a single pass unless NTRO explicitly accepts that interpretation.
  -  Keep adequate stand-off distance and motion parallax.

  -  Do not exceed lawful altitude/airspace limits.

 Metadata
  -  Start video before the useful pass and stop after it.
  -  Save SRT/tlog/DataFlash/ULog immediately.

  -  Note camera mode, stabilization, gimbal setting, and approximate time offset.
  -  Copy files with hashes.

 16. Security, privacy, and deployment


 NTRO and strategic mapping imply that offline operation is a product requirement, not a bonus.

 Minimum security controls
  -  No automatic upload to third-party AI or mapping services.
  -  Local model weights.
  -  Role-based access for projects.
  -  Audit log for upload, process, view, measure, and export.
  -  Checksums for inputs and outputs.
  -  Encryption in transit.
  -  Encrypted project storage where required.
  -  Configurable retention and secure deletion policy.
  -  Redaction of sensitive EXIF/path information in shared demo artifacts.

  -  No public repository containing raw operational data.
  -  Software bill of materials and licence manifest.
  -  Container image pinning and vulnerability scanning.

 Licence gate

 This is an engineering checklist, not legal advice. Before the finalist/deployment build, create a dependency and
 model-weight inventory and have the intended distribution reviewed.

 At minimum, record these facts:
  -  COLMAP's own library is BSD-licensed, but its official notice warns that separately licensed build dependencies can
     affect the resulting distribution.
  -  OpenDroneMap and OpenMVS use AGPLv3; modifying or serving AGPL-covered components can create
     source-availability obligations.
  -  CesiumJS is Apache 2.0 and supports offline/local data use.
  -  FFmpeg's effective licence depends on the codecs and build configuration included.
  -  AI code and AI checkpoints can have different licences. Record both.

  -  Dataset licences can restrict trained weights even when the training code is permissive.
  -  "Research use," "non-commercial," and "no military use" are unacceptable ambiguities for an NTRO-facing
     deployable core.

 Therefore:
 1.  Keep ODM as a separate baseline container, not proprietary core logic.
 2.  Prefer COLMAP dense/meshing or permissive components for the main distributable path.
 3.  Use only the explicitly Apache-licensed MapAnything checkpoint if that experiment is promoted.
 4.  Do not use original VGGT, DUSt3R, MASt3R, MASt3R-SLAM, or VGG-T³ weights in the deployable NTRO path
      without written clearance.
 5.  Store exact source revision, package version, checkpoint hash, licence URL, and intended-use decision in
      licenses.json.

 18. Innovation story for evaluators


 The innovation is not "we used AI to make 3D." Existing photogrammetry already makes 3D.

 Strong differentiators
 1.  Single-pass-aware keyframe intelligence - selects frames using parallax, overlap, blur, exposure, telemetry, and
      dynamic coverage.
 2.  Uncertainty-aware sensor fusion - visual geometry plus GPS/IMU priors weighted by quality.
 3.  Trustworthy AI assistance - learned masks, matches, and depth priors constrained by multi-view geometry.
 4.  Observed-versus-inferred provenance - prevents hallucinated surfaces from becoming "measurements."
 5.  Confidence-aware measurement - every measurement carries coverage and uncertainty evidence.
 6.  Operational offline product - air-gapped deployment, standard exports, auditability.
 7.  Self-evaluation - the output includes a quality report rather than only a model screenshot.

 One-line pitch

      We do not just reconstruct a scene; we tell the operator which parts are truly observed, which measurements are
      defensible, and where the single-pass data is insufficient.


 Demonstration sequence
 1.  Upload one video and SRT/telemetry.
 2.  Show automatic synchronization and a mapped flight path.
 3.  Show blur/redundancy rejection and dynamic masks.

 4.  Show camera trajectory and sparse cloud within the preview stage.
 5.  Reveal the dense georeferenced model.
 6.  Toggle confidence and provenance colors.
 7.  Measure a known roof/wall/road dimension.
 8.  Show error against an independent value.
 9.  Click an inferred/weak region and show the warning.
 10.   Show masked vs unmasked ghost artifacts.
 11.   Export LAZ/GLB/GeoTIFF/report.

 Questions judges are likely to ask
 How can you reconstruct an unseen facade? We cannot measure it. We either leave it unseen or generate a
 separately labelled inference that is excluded from verified measurement.

 Why is this better than COLMAP/WebODM? Those are geometry engines/baselines. Our system adds synchronized
 video/telemetry ingest, adaptive single-pass frame selection, dynamic suppression, uncertainty-aware fusion,
 provenance, measurement safeguards, self-evaluation, and an operational product workflow.

 Where is AI? In learned feature extraction/matching, temporal dynamic segmentation, constrained depth priors, and
 eventually calibrated confidence-not in untraceable generative hallucination.

 How do you get metric scale? From GPS/RTK camera positions and optional known scale, solved as a similarity
 alignment or a GPS-constrained bundle adjustment; we validate scale on independent dimensions.

 20. What should and should not be purchased

 Do not buy first
  - A new drone solely because it has better marketing claims.
  - An RTK kit before confirming access through the institute.
  - A huge workstation before proving the small pipeline.
  - Paid photogrammetry software as the core solution.
  - Cloud credits before the data and container are ready.

 Secure first
 1.  Authorized access to a suitable drone or dataset.
 2.  Authorized test site.
 3.  NVIDIA GPU worker.
 4.  Storage/backup.
 5.  Calibration target.
 6.  Ground-truth measurement tools.

 Budget tiers

 Bare-minimum research tier
  - Existing drone/public datasets.
  - Current Mac for development.
  - Borrowed/free NVIDIA compute.

  - Printed/rigid calibration board.
  - Tape/laser measurements.

 Risk: fragile availability and slow iteration.

 Practical SIH tier
  - Reliable drone access/rental.
  - Dedicated 24 GB GPU worker or dependable rental.
  - 1 TB scratch storage.
  - Laser distance meter.
  - Proper calibration target.

  - Backup power/network/storage plan.

 Advanced validation tier
  - RTK/PPK rig.
  - Survey-grade checkpoints/total station.
  - Reference LiDAR/CAD.
  - Air-gapped GPU server.

 This tier is not necessary to start, but it dramatically strengthens absolute-accuracy claims.

 21. Definition of done


 The prototype is SIH-ready only when all of these are true:

 Functional
  -  One command and the UI both process the canonical sample.
  -  Video and telemetry are synchronized visibly.
  -  Keyframes and masks are inspectable.
  -  Sparse and dense results are produced.
  -  Model is georeferenced and measured in metres.
  -  Flight path and source frames are preserved.
  -  Standard exports load in an independent tool.

 Accuracy/trust
  -  Independent checkpoint/measurement report exists.
  -  Relative, horizontal, and vertical errors are separate.
  -  Confidence layer is visible.
  -  Inferred surfaces are never labelled observed.
  -  Weak-region measurements warn the user.

  -  Baseline comparison exists.

 Reliability
  -  Clean deployment is documented.
  -  Runs are resumable/retryable.

  -  No internet is required for the demo.
  -  Failure messages are actionable.
  -  At least 10 fixed end-to-end test runs have been completed.

 Presentation
  -  Deterministic live demo under three minutes.
  -  Backup recording.
  -  Architecture, novelty, limitations, and metrics are explainable.
  -  Every technical claim has evidence.
  -  No fake "100% accuracy" or "perfect occlusion reconstruction" language appears in the deck.
